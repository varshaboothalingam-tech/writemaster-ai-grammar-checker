Grammar Rules Quick Reference

A comprehensive guide to common grammar rules and writing best practices.

Subject-Verb Agreement

Singular subjects take singular verbs:

She goes to school. He likes coffee.

She go to school. He like coffee.

Third-person singular (he/she/it) requires verb with -s or -es.

Plural subjects take plural verbs:

They are happy. We have books.

They is happy. We has books.

Plural subjects use "are" and "have".

Use "doesn't" with third-person singular:

He doesn't like it. She doesn't know.

He don't like it. She don't know.

"Don't" is for I/you/we/they; "doesn't" is for he/she/it.

Verb Tenses

After "didn't", use the base form:

I didn't go. She didn't eat.

I didn't went. She didn't ate.

The past tense is already in "didn't".

After modal verbs, use the base form:

He can sing. She will go.

He can sings. She will goes.

Modal verbs: can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must.

After am/is/are, use the -ing form:

I am going. She is running.

I am go. She is run.

Present continuous requires -ing form.

Pronouns

Use "I" (not "me") as a subject:

My friend and I went to the store.

My friend and me went to the store.

Put yourself last: "My friend and I", not "I and my friend".

"I" is always capitalized:

I am a student. I went home.

i am a student. i went home.

The pronoun "I" should always be uppercase.

Comparisons

Use "than" for comparisons:

She is taller than me.

She is taller then me.

"Than" is for comparisons; "then" is for time/sequence.

Use "there are" with plural nouns:

There are many books.

There is many books.

"There is" for singular, "there are" for plural.

Punctuation

Start sentences with capitals, end with punctuation:

Hello, how are you?

hello how are you

Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with . ? or !

Proper contractions:

I'm happy. Don't do that. It's a nice day.

im happy. dont do that. its a nice day.

Use apostrophes in contractions. "It's" = "it is"; "its" = possessive.

Spelling

Common spelling mistakes:

definately -> definitely

seperate -> separate

occured -> occurred

recieve -> receive

Double-check commonly misspelled words.

Writing Tips
Keep Sentences Concise

Aim for 15-20 words per sentence. Break long sentences into shorter ones.

Avoid Filler Words

Remove words like "basically", "actually", "literally", "just", "really" that add no meaning.

Use Active Voice

"The teacher graded the papers" is stronger than "The papers were graded by the teacher."

Vary Your Vocabulary

Replace weak words (good, bad, big, small) with more specific alternatives.

Read Aloud

Reading your text aloud helps catch awkward phrasing and run-on sentences.
